## Initial source review - 2026-10-28

**Media extraction needs explicit nested-source provenance and isolated credential caches.** Reviewed commit `f264a6cbd1f630894da0f368df00f8aae50140e0` (commit date `2026-10-26T22:07:14Z`). The enumerated snapshot contains 1235 tracked paths.

The inspected built-in PO-token cache uses a shared process-global dictionary with locked get/store/delete, default capacity 25 and LRU ordering refreshed on a hit. Expired entries are removed on access and an expired replacement does not overwrite a still-valid entry. Equality at the expiry second remains accepted because expiry tests use strictly less-than. The cap limits entry count, not bytes; keys and values are trusted here, and identity/tenant binding belongs to callers not inspected in this cache. Fixtures cover expiry, replacement, eviction and sharing, but not concurrent initialization or a full extraction lifecycle.

The Embedly extractor unwraps nested source URLs and passes the wrapper as referer to the delegated extractor; the Google video-search generator paginates until a page lacks its next marker, with no local page cap in that function. This reinforces the need to preserve wrapper-to-final-source provenance and impose host-level URL, redirect, result and byte budgets. The cached JS-challenge provider documentation marks most internals unstable and includes an illustrative shell=True command built from challenge/player text and logged in full. That example is not evidence that the built-in provider executes this way, and it must not be copied into a trusted adapter.
